# Design

full-self-working (fsw) is one local process that sells, quotes, delivers and learns on freelance marketplaces for one operator. This file is the contract between modules; AGENTS.md holds the rules every change keeps. A module marked with a later phase does not exist yet; the phase that builds it adds its section here.

## The loop

Per enabled platform, one surface session per tick: confirm the login (a surface with `publicSense` keeps sensing when the login fails), sense the list rows (money fields go to the deal state machine), triage each row, read and decide and authorize and plan at most MAX_THREADS_PER_PLATFORM opened threads, apply the deal effects, then drain the outbox inside the outbound hours with read-back, so a deposit request from a hire seen in this tick is drained in the same tick; six failed polls in a row notify once. An item parked from its list row (closed, past the reply window, or labelled with a service the catalog does not hold or the instance does not answer there) waits until a newer message arrives or the settings it was parked under change (the services the instance answers, the reply windows, or how the platform's labels map onto the catalog), which checks it again from its list row. An item a pass already read (observed at night, or decided with nothing to send) keeps the list time it covered the same way, so a platform notice that moves the list time past the newest stored message does not send it through the model on every tick; a night read holds only for night ticks, so the first tick inside the outbound hours still decides. Daily and hourly jobs keep their ids in `run/state.json` lastRuns.

## Triage

`triage(inbound, ledgerRow, {mode, now, settings, descriptor, openOutbox, parkKey})` decides each list row from the row and the ledger alone: `wait` (unchanged since the last pass, or an outbox message or quote still `planned`, `awaiting_operator`, `approved` or `sending` holds the thread), `park` (an unread platform notice, a closed request, a message past `pipeline.replyWindowHours`, a label outside the catalog or a service `settings.answered` leaves out; counted as `thread.system`, `thread.skipped` or `thread.operator`), `observe` (observe mode) or `open`. A park and a read carry the list time and the settings key they were made under (`meta.skipped`, `meta.settled`); a thread whose pass threw is passed again while `meta.failed.count` is under `THREAD_ATTEMPTS` (3) and nothing went out since. `awaitingReply` is derived: newest message inbound, nothing observed after it, inside the window.

## Authorize

`authorize(item, ctx)` is the one decision on whether an outbound item may leave, composed in this order: the gate with its slop lint (an approved text that fails it is `refuse`), mode and outbound hours, what the model decided (unsupported, label mismatch, a service no longer answered, needsOperator, a stage after the hire while `orders.watched` is false, confidence under `pipeline.minAutoConfidence`), operator-only kinds (`pay_request`, `delivery`), the price row, a stated budget under the cost floor (`budget.below_floor`) and the Seoul day's cash caps. An operator approval answers every decision rule. `mode.observe`, `hours.closed` and `budget.cash` are holds: the verdict is `send` and the outbox leaves the row as it is. A cash quote refused only by the day's caps is held with nobody asked and goes out on a later day's budget; one no day can pay (no caps, an unread cost, a cost over the cap) goes to the operator. `operatingMode(configMode, {forceMode, now, instance})` is the one reading of the forced mode and the outbound hours for both the loop and `authorize`. Pacing stays in the driver guard; the drain treats `FSW_PACING_LIMIT` as a refusal before the page.

## Outbox

Statuses move only along `TRANSITIONS` (`src/outbox/plan.mjs`): `planned` to `awaiting_operator`, `sending`, `blocked` or `stale`; `awaiting_operator` to `approved`, `rejected` or `stale`; `approved` to `sending`, `blocked` or `stale`; `sending` to `observed` or `unobserved`, or back to `planned` or `approved` when the surface refused before the page; `unobserved` leaves only by the operator (`fsw approve <id> --sent` or `--reject`). Each change is a compare-and-set with one `outbox.status` event. `drain` first reads back every `sending` row and never sends it again (a quote and a proposal have no read-back and go to `unobserved`), then screens planned rows (`operator` to `awaiting_operator` with an approval id, `refuse` to `blocked`) and sends the rows `authorize` lets go now, oldest first, claiming each with the compare-and-set; the first cash quote the day cannot pay holds every newer one. A row is `observed` only on an observed receipt, and only then is the outbound message written and the deal signal returned. A quote the surface pressed but could not confirm keeps `receipt.pressed` and counts against its day's caps until settled. Known gap: the `authorized` token handed to the surface is the authorize result itself, bound to no row or text, until wp9's `src/core/authz.mjs`.

## Deal

`advance(ledger, signal, ctx)` is the only writer of `jobs.status`, `quotes.status`, `payments`, `reviews` and `money_events`, one transaction per signal, and returns effects (`plan_outbox`, `close_outbox`, `start_run`, `record_case`, `sync_portfolio`, `notify`) the loop carries out after the commit. A job opens at `quoted` from the first observed quote; a hire or an escrowed order moves it to `ordered` (on soomgo with a deposit request for the operator); a paid deposit starts the run, or for an operator-delivered service hands the job to the operator (`in_progress` with the balance request as the schedule-the-sessions item); `run_started`, `run_accepted` and `run_failed` come from the job child; `handoff_done` comes from `fsw job handoff <id> <step> [file ...]`, which copies the files into `jobs/<id>/handoff/<step>/`, and the delivery (with the balance request) is planned only once every user-handoff step of the last run that declares `produces` (a build, a URL file, an import report) is recorded with its files, or without files when its `when` condition does not hold; it carries those files, and its approval reason lists the steps that produce nothing for the operator to confirm there; `delivery_observed` makes it `delivered` and asks for portfolio permission; a settled job closes (`job_closed` case, portfolio sync); a first revision request reopens it. Money signals are recorded once per key, older than seven days as history, and a chat row still showing a recorded hire adds nothing. A signal the state does not accept is a `deal.refused` event. `sweep` re-derives the outbox effects a crash between the commit and the effect applier lost; `readiness` holds a delivery until its payment level is met and stales a payment request no longer due.

## Work

`runJob(jobId, ctx)` runs one paid job through its recipe with the executors it is handed (codex or jwc), the deal's payment check and a report door, writes `jobs/<id>/brief.md`, runs acceptance with one retry and reports `run_started` and `run_accepted` (with the recipe's user-handoff steps) or `run_failed`; it writes no job status. `startDue` spawns paid and revision jobs up to `jobs.maxParallel` and never an operator-delivered one; `src/loop/job.mjs` is the job child's composition.

## Loop step

`step(platform, ctx)` runs one platform pass: login; sense, with every list row's money handed to the deal; triage; while the Seoul day's budget pays no quote, a buyer request on a cash platform is left unread with no model call (`threads.budget`); read, decide and plan at most `MAX_THREADS_PER_PLATFORM` opened threads (a buyer request ruled unsupported plans nothing and counts as `thread.operator`); the deal effects; then the drain (outside observe mode) and the effects of what it observed.

Version 4 adds the outbox and changes no older table, so the 2c77326 code opens a version 4 file.

Every SQL statement on the outbox lives in `src/core/ledger.mjs`: `addOutbox`, `updateOutbox(id, patch, {from})` (with `from`, a compare-and-set on status that returns null when the row has moved), `getOutbox`, `outboxByKey`, `outboxByApproval` and `listOutbox({platform, statuses, kinds, threadId, jobId})`; only `src/outbox` and `importApprovals` call the two writers. The outbox key is `platform:threadRef:kind:sha1(text|amount|files)` of the planned item (`outboxKey`), so an item planned twice is one row. An operator edit stays on its row as `operator.finalText` with `operator.edited: true`, and the drain sends and reads back `operator.finalText ?? payload.text`; the key keeps hashing the planned text, so the brain planning the same draft again lands on the same row, and a revised draft is a new row. A payment row is written only from evidence. `importApprovals(ledger, dir)` turns each 2c77326 approval file into an outbox row keyed by approval_id (open to awaiting_operator, approved, rejected and dropped to rejected, sent to observed, expired to stale, blocked, unobserved; project_proposal to proposal) and reports a file it cannot map by name; a row still planned, awaiting_operator or approved whose file holds a final status follows the file with a compare-and-set and an `outbox.status` event (`reconciled`); a second run imports and reconciles nothing.

## Surface

`openSurface(platform, {session, record, replay})` returns, per platform this build can work (`hasSurface`: kmong, soomgo), `login`, `sense` (list rows as Inbound, nothing opened), `read(ref, {willReply, kind})` (a Thread; `kind` is 'message' or 'request', a soomgo request is one inbound message built from its answers, and a kind the platform lacks is refused before any page loads), `send` and `quote` (a Receipt, refused without `authorized.verdict === 'send'`; the token is not yet bound to its outbox row, a gap wp9 closes with `src/core/authz.mjs`), `payRequest` (soomgo, needs `approved`), `orders` (`{counts, total, gap, orders}`), `supply` (a `Supply`) and `stop`; soomgo also has `spend` (the Seoul day's cash charges by source and every smart quote campaign with its daily budget, one page each); kmong also has `propose` (Proposals) and `signIn`, the email login the supervisor uses when kmong signed out. Page knowledge stays in `src/platforms/<id>`; the surface maps shapes. `openListingSurface('kmong')` serves the gig writer. A session may be the signed-in Chrome, a recording (`record`: one JSON line per call, `{seq, op, name, arg, answer | error, at}`, under `<instance>/qa/surfaces/<platform>/<day>/`) or a replay of one, which fails with `FSW_REPLAY_DIVERGED` on the first call that differs. `scripts/surface-drift.mjs` compares a recording's answer shapes with `test/fixtures/surfaces/<platform>/shapes.json`; `scripts/probe.mjs` runs one read operation inside the window after the live tick of a 5-minute mark. `SAMPLERS` (kmong, soomgo, freelancer, wishket, fiverr) is the sampler map `sampleMarket` is handed.

## Policy gate

`gateOutbound({platform, kind, text, attachments, amountKRW, thread}, {descriptor, instance, priceRow, lessonsRules})` returns every violation with its rule id: contact (phone, e-mail, URL, messenger ID and payout account unless the descriptor allows that class at the thread's stage), identity (where `contactPolicy.identity` is forbidden), payment (off-platform wording and the payout account), price (outside the row's floor and ceiling), claims (an experience number not in `profile.approvedClaims`, anything in `claimsNotToMake`), slop (floor findings of `lintText` block, reflex findings warn), shape (length within `descriptor.limits`, the customer's language, no empty or duplicate message) and learned rules from `lessons/gate-rules.json`. `authorize` composes it with the rest of the send rules (Authorize).

## Economics

Prices are market based. A (platform, service) row's market base comes from the newest summary: under 5 reviews p25 + 0.35 (median - p25), 5 to 29 the median, 30 or more halfway to p75; serviceFit multiplies it (strong 1.15, some 1.0, none 0.9). The cost floor is the smallest gross whose net after fees covers the standard package's agent hours at `yield.agentHourCostKRW` plus its operator minutes at `yield.humanHourValueKRW`; the base is the higher of the two, and a row is viable while its cost floor stays at or under the market ceiling. Packages are base, base x 2.2 and base x 4, rounded to 1,000 KRW. A request quote is the guide average times a complexity factor (0.6 to 1.6), clamped to the guide, moved toward the buyer's stated budget and never below the cost floor plus the quote cost. `splitPayment` gives deposit and balance below `payment.milestoneThresholdKRW` and milestones above it, through the mechanism the descriptor names.

`sampleMarket(instance, {platforms, samplers, withPage, loadFx, now, day, log})` samples every platform the config lists with the samplers and page opener it is given, retries a failed category once, stops a platform at its first bot challenge, converts foreign prices at the day's ECB rates or fails that platform by name, replaces only the pairs it read, carries pairs it could not read for up to `CARRY_DAYS` (7), and writes the day's files only when the summary changed. Each platform is read through its market engine (`config.market.engines.<platform>`, else the descriptor's `market.engine`, else `owned`; `attached` is the operator's Chrome for public pages, `archive` the newest Wayback capture dated by its capture time), at most `config.market.categoriesPerRun.<platform>` categories a run, stalest first, and an error marked final is not retried; `config.market.cadence.<platform>` puts a platform in the daily market job or the hourly slice. `answeredServices(config, platform)` is the list of catalog services an instance answers on a platform (`platforms.<id>.services`, null for every agent-delivered one), and `operatorDelivered(service)` marks a service the operator delivers, such as `lesson_tutoring`. A price guide that prints only its average prices at that average. `refreshPriceTable(instance, {adapterFor, day, log})` prices every platform with summary entries and writes a new table only when a row changed; `latestTable(dir)` reads the newest. `catalogServicesFor(descriptor, label)` is the one mapping from a platform's label to catalog services.

## Scheduling

All marks are Seoul time and aligned to minute 0, and the 14 job ids of `src/supervisor/schedule.mjs` are the `lastRuns` keys of `run/state.json`, unchanged from 2c77326 and 9101c1c so a takeover reads the old state as it is. Every 5 minutes: `poll`, one loop step per platform in draft or auto mode, because marketplaces rank sellers by response time and a buyer request closes after its first few quotes; then `orders`, which applies the effects a crash lost (the deal's `sweep`) and starts paid and revision jobs within `jobs.maxParallel`. Hourly at :00: `hourly` (supervisor self-check, the read pass on observe platforms and the lead supply report), `projects` (크몽 엔터프라이즈 projects, since a project stays open for days and its proposal quota is per day), `listings` (kmong review results) and `market-hour` (the market slice of hour-cadence platforms, one Fiverr search). Daily at 00:00: `market`, `price`, `allocation` (no arm for a service the instance does not answer there), `portfolio`, `listings-refresh` (gigs whose top items changed, and the kmong career figure), `publish` (skipped and kept due under a forced observe pass) and `learn`. Daily at 09:00: `digest`, which opens with the lead supply section. A job is retired only by the founder in writing.

The supervisor is one process started by a per-user scheduled task at logon and re-checked hourly at :00; a pid lock prevents doubles. It keeps the BrowserSkill daemon alive as a child, which ends with the supervisor that started it, so every tick with due jobs checks the keeper before its first job and a supervisor that took over after a code change starts its own daemon at its first tick. It holds a Windows execution-state request so the machine does not idle into sleep, runs platform pipelines concurrently with one tab per platform, and runs jobs as separate processes up to `jobs.maxParallel`. Outbound actions respect `activeHours` and each descriptor's pacing (minimum gap between page loads, maximum actions and messages per hour).

`fsw run` and `fsw tick` share the pid lock at `<instance>/run/supervisor.pid`: one process per instance drains the outbox, so a tick takes the lock for its pass or exits 1 while a live supervisor holds it. At start, before its first tick, the supervisor imports the approval files the 2c77326 code wrote (`importApprovals`) and logs the result. A tick that wakes less than a second before the mark it slept toward runs at that mark. A platform that signed out is signed in again from the file `logins` names (an email login per platform, kept outside the repository) through the surface's `signIn`, at most once an hour, keyed on the last `platform.sign_in` event, which records only whether it worked. Every line of `supervisor.log` is `<ISO time> <text>`: `supervisor pid <n>`, `approvals import read <n>, imported <n>, already <n>, reconciled <n>, skipped <n>`, `tick <job>:<status> ... failures=<n>`, `code moved from <a7> to <b7>; restarting` and `supervisor exiting for a restart` or `supervisor stopped`; the `supervisor.tick` event names the commit the process runs (`code`).

A process keeps the modules it loaded, and agents edit the main checkout all day, so the scheduled task runs the supervisor from a detached worktree of `main` (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\fsw\code`, or `FSW_CODE_DIR`) that only ever holds committed code and never runs npm, so nothing `src/cli/run.mjs` loads may import a package (`scripts/check-imports.mjs`). After each tick the supervisor compares that worktree with `main`; when `main` moved and the new commit's module graph imports in a temporary worktree, it checks the commit out and exits with code 75, and the launcher starts it again at once. A commit that does not import is reported once and the running code stays. The launcher restarts a supervisor that crashed after five minutes, and the config is read again every tick, so a setting changes at the next mark without a restart.

A takeover of the live line, and a way back from it, run only through tracked scripts, each read-only unless given `--apply` or its write flag. `scripts/takeover-check.mjs` confirms that local `main`, the deploy worktree and the expected commit agree, that the lock holder is a live supervisor (its start time when the lock records one) and the task runs, that no gap between ticks since a given time (the newest tick before it included) passes a limit, and that `supervisor.log` holds exactly the handovers, restarts and approval imports expected. `scripts/ledger-snapshot.mjs` takes one consistent read of the ledger beside a record of its hash, integrity, version and table counts, checks a ledger against that record, and restores a snapshot only inside the instance it names while no supervisor runs and the task is stopped, keeping the replaced file with its WAL under names SQLite opens. `scripts/approvals-writeback.mjs` writes the outbox outcomes back into the approval files the 2c77326 code reads, planning every file before it writes any and refusing while a supervisor runs, so the old code never sends an item v0 already sent and still holds the items v0 planned. `scripts/rollback-takeover.mjs` stops the task and the lock holder, writes back, moves `main` to the old line with a compare-and-swap, starts the old line and waits for its first tick; until the write-back and the move both finished it restarts v0 instead and exits 2, since starting the old code over stale files would send twice. `scripts/rehearsal-effects.mjs` proves that a rehearsal on a copy of the instance sent nothing. The takeover of 2026-10-26 moved `main` from the 2c77326 line (kept as `archive/20261026-151909`) to v0 with a compare-and-swap, ran the rollback once as a drill and moved forward again, recorded the ledger event `takeover`, and replaced `origin/main` with a lease; from then on `main` moves only by fast-forward to `v0`.

## Lead supply

The hourly job writes `operator/supply/supply-<YYYYMMDDHH>.json`, one file per Seoul hour, with one `SupplyReport` per enabled platform: `listings` (published kmong gigs by the review state the listings job last saw), `services` (offered services, and those without request items that the instance answers there, read from the surface's `supply()`, 003's `Supply`), `parked` (distinct threads parked over the last seven days as out of catalog, stale, expired or notice), `candidates` (the five platform labels with the most threads parked over those seven days because no catalog service covers them, with their thread counts, read against the current mapping so a label mapped since drops out: the queue of services to add), `spend` (where the surface has `spend()`: the Seoul day's cash charges by source, fsw's quotes, the platform's smart quotes and other, and every automatic campaign with its daily budget, against `platforms.<id>.platformSpendMaxPerDay`; a budget over it or unreadable is told to the operator once a Seoul day), `awaitingReply` (threads whose newest message is inbound and inside the reply window) and `waiting` (outbox rows awaiting the operator or approved). A read that fails leaves a partial report with its reason, and the file is still written. It says why nothing sells before anyone suspects the loop. The 09:00 digest opens with these lines and its notice starts with them; its approvals count reads the outbox.

## Proposals

The projects job reads, judges and drafts 크몽 엔터프라이즈 proposals within the day's quota and plans one outbox row of kind `proposal` per draft it keeps, `decision.needsOperator` false when the send policy allows it; it never sends. The kmong poll's drain screens the rows with `authorize` and sends them through `Surface.propose`, which reads the project, fills the form through the projects page module, reads it back and looks for the proposal on `/seller/proposals`. A spent quota and a missing enterprise profile come back as refusals the drain returns as they were; the next projects run carries what the drain sent onto the project threads (`project.proposed`, dated by the job's clock), remembers a missing profile for the day and turns a row whose project closed stale. Only `src/surface/kmong.mjs` and `src/outbox/drain.mjs` call `.propose(`.

## Settings

`src/core/config.schema.json` (JSON Schema 2020-12) owns every config key's type, allowed values, default and meaning; `x-choice` marks a choice the operator makes before the platform it guards acts (`platforms.<id>.mode`, `maxCashPerDay`, `services`, `bidBudget`). `examples/instance/config.json` is generated from its defaults (`scripts/gen-example-config.mjs`, checked in `npm run check`). `fsw settings` lists every key with its value, default and meaning; `fsw settings set <key> <value>` validates the whole new config with ajv, writes it beside `config.json` and renames it over, and records one `settings.changed` event; `fsw settings reset <key>` restores the default; `fsw doctor` warns once per unmade choice. ajv loads only inside `validateConfig`, which only `fsw settings set` and `reset` call. A career figure a listing prints is derived from the profile's dated roles (`careerMonths` and `careerLabel` in `src/core/profile.mjs`: the lower value of every coarse period, hidden when a period does not parse).

## Operator CLI

`bin/fsw.mjs` maps each verb to `src/cli/<file>.mjs`: `init`, `profile`, `doctor`, `settings`, `status` (instance, ledger and outbox counts, the last tick, the enabled platforms), `tick [--once] [--only job,job]`, `run [--once]`, `schedule`, `approve`, `deal` (portfolio permission and reviews the operator saw, sent to the deal as signals), `job`, `probe`, `market`, `price`, `publish`, `listings`, `projects`, `services` and `edits`. A verb holds no domain logic; it calls the module that owns it.
